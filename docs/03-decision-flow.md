# Chapter 3 — The Month-by-Month Decision Flow

*Turn "should I pay?" into a month of small, honest observations.*

This is the heart of the manual. One month of light note-taking replaces months of wondering. The routine costs about five minutes a day in weeks one and two, and almost nothing after that. No spreadsheets required (though one is fine if you like them) — **a single note file plus this chapter** is all you need.

## Before the month starts: the baseline log

Create one note (paper, phone app, or a plain text file) with five blanks:

```text
Month of: _______
Provider/tool used: _______
Free-tier plan: _______
What I use it for: _______
The moment I had to wait/reroute/downgrade because of a limit: (tally)
```

That last line — **the tally of limit-moments** — is the whole experiment.

## Week 1 — Use it normally, tally honestly

No changes to your habits. The only job: whenever you hit the ceiling, make one tally mark and (if you can spare ten seconds) note what you were doing.

What counts as a **limit-moment**:

- "You've reached your message limit" (or you notice your own pace grinding down hourly)
- A file that wouldn't upload, or a document that got half-ignored
- A feature you needed was greyed out / marked upgrade
- Peak-hours slowdown made you stop and come back later
- The model made a mess of a *hard* task and you couldn't tell if it was the free model or you

What doesn't count (don't tally these):

- General "hmm, the answer wasn't great" on easy tasks (happens on paid tiers too)
- A limit you paid no actual price for (hit a cap in the evening, were done anyway)
- Curiosity about a feature you haven't missed yet

## Week 2 — Use it normally *plus* the natural-workload test

Same tallying, but now deliberately run your **hardest real workloads** through the free tier at least three times each. If you're a writer, one long-chapter edit. If you're a business owner, one full email-triage or budgeting session. This is calibration — you need to see the *wanted* work hitting the ceiling, not just the background chatter.

Keep notes on:

- **When** you hit the cap (morning mid-task = serious; 11 PM free time = not)
- **How often** (daily? twice this week? fine — that's a real number)
- **The bypass that came next** (waited an hour? switched to another free tool? gave up on the task? The nature of your bypass is a hidden price tag.)

## Week 3 — Interpret the data, candidly

Take ten quiet minutes and answer five questions:

1. **How many limit-moments did I tally?** (A number <= 5 for the month is a different world than 5 in one day.)
2. **Was I usually in the middle of something that mattered?** Or at loose ends?
3. **What did my bypasses cost me?** Fifteen minutes of waiting is tolerable; a lost train of thought isn't. Murked-up hard-task quality is often the biggest hidden cost of staying free.
4. **Which *feature* did I reach for and not have?** (Deep research? Whole-document upload?) Feature-ceiling paywalls are the most legitimate pay reasons.
5. **Which *role* would paid fill that free doesn't?** Write the sentence in one line: "Paid tier would let me ___."

## Week 4 (or when the trial exists) — The decision

Two decision paths, both fine:

### Path A: Stay free (the common ending)

You'll stay free if the tally is low or the bypasses were painless. That's not a consolation prize. Prescribe yourself:

- A second tool on another free tier for the specific job where tool #1 pinches (a "buddy system" — free cohorts are generous to people with two or three accounts across providers)
- A note of the *one* thing that would tip you over, so you'll recognize the day it happens
- Re-evaluation in 3 months (calendars aren't just for renewals)

### Path B: Try paid (with trial or the cheapest month)

Go into Chapter 4's trial checklist. Pay monthly, never annually, until the *monthly* decision has been yes three separate months running. Annual is a discount for commitment you haven't yet earned evidence for.

## The 90-day loop

Simplest version of the whole chapter as a recurring cadence:

```text
Month 1: Tally limit-moments (Weeks 1–2), interpret (Week 3), decide (Week 4)
  ├─> Stay free: set the "tipping-point" note; re-run in 3 months
  └─> Try paid: run the Month-2 trial checklist
        ├─> Paid earned it: keep it, set a quarterly renewal review
        └─> Paid didn't earn it: Chapter 5, downgrade with dignity
```

## Two important framing notes

**Competition exists for your benefit.** AI providers are in a knife-fight for consumers, and free tiers keep getting *more generous* over time, not less. The default trend is that "free" keeps absorbing workloads that were paid-tier-only two years ago. Staying free is not a compromise; most months it's the rational outcome.

**The cap you pay for is real but finite.** Even paid tiers meter you (research/usage caps, fair-use throttling). If your Month-2 trial hits *paid-tier* caps too, that's data pointing to the API/high tier — or to pacing expectations rather than budgets.
