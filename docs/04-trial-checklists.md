# Chapter 4 — Trial Checklists

*Evidence collected in the trial window beats promises made on the pricing page.*

Most paid AI tiers offer a **free trial or a first-month promo** — and a first *paid* month (usually ~$15–25, fully monthly cancellable) doubles as a trial with better data. Whichever door you walk in, the point is the same: **run a test plan, not free-floating curiosity.** This chapter gives you that plan, sized for one week of normal life.

## Before the trial starts (30 minutes of prep)

- [ ] **Cancel anytime now?** Find the cancellation button *before* your trial starts and take a screenshot, so future-you won't be hunting under deadline. (Chapter 5 has the full walkthrough.)
- [ ] **Mark the trial end date** in a real calendar with a "cancel-or-keep, $N" note. Push the renewal day into tomorrow's problem, not next month's.
- [ ] **Write your one-line hypothesis** in the note file from Chapter 3:

  > "I asked to pay so that I can __________, because free ______________ last month."

  Example: *"I asked to pay so that I can run deep-research on quarterly market scans, because free ran out of my 5-hour cap twice last week."* If it doesn't fit into one sentence, you don't know what you're reviewing yet.

- [ ] **Same workload, same tasks as Week 2.** The trial is now a controlled experiment, not a new toy. New toys always *feel* worth it.

## The checklist: seven days, real workloads only

### Day 1 — Cap and ceiling tour (upgraded from Chapter 1)

- [ ] Which model do I get by default now, and has anything obvious improved? (Not identical questions!)
- [ ] What are the *paid-tier* caps? Every tier still has ceilings — find yours on day one so it doesn't ambush you in a meeting.
- [ ] Do the features I paid for actually exist in MY plan — including whether any are still greyed out? ("Pro Plus" sneaks in above "Pro.")
- [ ] Confirm your hypothesis feature works at least once today.

### Days 2–3 — Loving it? Suspicious? Both are data

- [ ] Does the flagship model actually deliver noticeably on *your real hard tasks* (long edits, messy files, tricky multi-step problems)?
- [ ] Try your hardest real workload from last month through paid: the exact thing that let you down on free. Does it behave better, and how gracefully does it fail?
- [ ] Skip free-floating chats ("ask the AI random things") — they never answer the question you came here to answer.

### Days 4–5 — File and volume pressure test

- [ ] Your biggest real document/repo/budget upload — does it work on paid where free said no?
- [ ] Your most typical full work-session — did you finish without a whisper of a cap?
- [ ] The peak-hour test (if relevant to your schedule): does your plan match your working hours, or is priority access true only during hours you don't work? If paid is no faster at the times *you* actually use it, some of what you paid for is on vacation.

### Day 6 — Workflow and memory check

- [ ] Long conversation memory: does a long session keep context where free kept forgetting? Longer memory is often a genuinely better day-to-day upgrade than raw speed.
- [ ] Workspace/project features (if in my tier): did they change *how* I work, or just get opened once?
- [ ] Integration round: any tools/apps you actually use — does paid tier enable integrations that matter, or is your need theoretical?

### Day 7 — The verdict

- [ ] Answer literally, with numbers: how many real limit-moments did paid prevent this week? (From a tally, not a feeling.)
- [ ] What did my paid tier do that **my Week 2 free log** was the reason I wanted it? Name the specific moment.
- [ ] Anything I'm paying for that I never used?
- [ ] **Keep / Cancel / Downgrade within the tiers** (this last one is more common than people realize — a mid-tier may be the right fit).

## Interpretation rules (how not to fool yourself)

**Keep.** The evidence for keeping is when paid removed a *specific* pain: "I hit no caps in 5 work sessions I previously capped in two," "my 300-page PDF got analyzed in one go," "deep-research mode replaced four hours of tab-hopping."

**Cancel even if you liked the tool.** If you can point to *no* moment where paid did something free didn't — and the same is true again after the 90-day check — free is fully sufficient. Paying a website monthly because it felt nice is a gym-membership mistake with worse weather excuses. Liking it isn't evidence you needed it.

**Downgrade within the same company.** If a cheaper tier covers the features you actually used and it wasn't the flagship model you were missing, move one tier down rather than all the way to free. Partial pay is a real option often overlooked.

## Trial hygiene: three traps to avoid

1. **Recency bias with the new-toy sparkle.** First days are buzz; run your hardest workload *again* on day 5–6 when the sparkle has worn off.
2. **The forgotten auto-renewal.** Trial-to-paid conversion silently happens when the end-date note was never put in a calendar. Calendar notes work; screenshots work.
3. **The annual switcheroo.** Paid tiers steer you toward annual pricing ("2 months free!") — fun if you already use it monthly, but never commit to annual before your *monthly* decision has been "yes" once, and ideally multiple times across a quarter.

## After a trial, one clean archive step

Whatever you decide — save, in your Chapter 3 note file, the outcome and the reason in a single line, like a reminder to future-you:

```text
[Month] [Provider] [Plan] Trial: KEPT — deep-research saved ~3 hrs/week of real work
[Month] [Provider] [Plan] Trial: CANCELED — never used deep-research after Day 2
[Month] [Provider] [Plan] Nope — hit paid caps in Days 4-5, need API tier instead
```

This is how decisions compound instead of repeating for years. (It's the same trick we recommend in sibling guides — decisions are cheap when you write them down.)
